# Player Manual

## Your role

You lead a transformation over **4 quarters**. Each quarter your team decides where to invest, how to govern, how to answer the quarter's crisis, and must convince an executive committee with conflicting interests. After quarter 4 your team gets a **verdict** (Victory, Partial success or Defeat) and a **grade** from A+ to F.

## The screen

| Area | What it is for |
| --- | --- |
| **Metrics bar** | Cash, debt, velocity, trust, resilience, compliance. Hover a label for its definition. |
| **Win conditions** | The 7 objectives to reach by the end, green when met today. The projected verdict shows where you stand. |
| **3D map** | The elements of the organisation (systems, sites, centres…) and their state. Red = critical. |
| **Initiative portfolio** | Possible investments, their cost, effects and duration. |
| **Governance & crisis** | The quarter's governance posture and your answer to the crisis. |
| **War Room** | 1-on-1 meetings and the board. |
| **History** | The report of each quarter played. |

## One quarter, step by step

1. **Analyse** the 3D map and metrics: where are the weak spots?
2. **Negotiate** 1-on-1: learn what each executive wants, sign pacts if needed.
3. **Pitch** your strategy to the board: its vote sets your quarter's **mandate**.
4. **Choose** your initiatives (2 at most, 3 if the board approved).
5. **Set** your governance posture and your crisis response.
6. **Submit** your decisions. The facilitator (or you, in solo play) resolves the quarter.
7. **Read** the report: effects, incidents, executive reactions.

## Rules you need to know

- **Budget**: the quarter's commitments (initiatives, crisis response, pacts) must fit in the cash available.
- **Capacity**: at most 2 initiatives per quarter. The board mandate can raise it to 3 or cut it to 1.
- **One-time initiatives**: each initiative can be bought once. Some take 2 quarters: cost is paid at start, effects land on delivery.
- **Run budget**: the business funds your recurring costs at their starting level. Costs above it come out of your cash; if you cut them, you keep half of the savings.
- **Debt compounds**: it grows every quarter, faster with lax governance and fast delivery.
- **Compliance**: below 50%, fines apply.
- **Insolvency**: negative cash hits everyone's trust and prevents victory.
- **Incidents**: very fragile elements can fail; each incident costs money and velocity.

## The executives

Each executive has a **personality**, a **bias**, a **hidden agenda** and **priorities**. They judge your proposals by their interests, not by a "right answer".

- **Patience**: each rejected, empty or repeated proposal uses it up. At zero they refuse to meet until next quarter and vote against you on the board. It recovers 50 points each quarter.
- **Pacts**: when an executive demands a concession, you can sign it with a committed budget. The pact is honoured at quarter end: cost charged, trust up.
- **Board**: all members vote at once, then debate. The vote sets the quarter's mandate:
  - **Approved**: +1 initiative and +5 velocity;
  - **Conditional quorum**: EXTREME-risk initiatives blocked;
  - **Rejected**: -1 initiative, HIGH and EXTREME-risk initiatives blocked.

## How to win

The verdict counts **7 objectives**: debt, trust, velocity, resilience, total cost, elements modernised, positive cash.

- **Victory**: all 7 met. A+ with a wide margin, A otherwise.
- **Partial success**: positive cash and at least 4 objectives met.
- **Defeat**: otherwise.

## Tips

- Prepare the board 1-on-1: a proposal that speaks to one executive will be rejected by the others.
- A quantified proposal (amount, deadline, milestone, metric) beats an intention.
- Shortcuts (bypassing controls, forced throughput) pay in quarter 1 and cost a lot later.
- Keep cash for crises: one comes every quarter.
