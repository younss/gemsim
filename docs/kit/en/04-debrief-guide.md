# Debrief Guide

The debrief is where learning happens. Allow **40 minutes**. Project the cockpit (*Executive Debrief* tab): rankings, radar and quarter history.

## 1. Decompression (5 min)

- "In one word, how do you feel?"
- "Which moment surprised you most?"

## 2. What happened (10 min)

Ask each team to tell its story, with numbers (History tab):

- "What was your strategy in quarter 1? Did you keep it?"
- "Which quarter tipped your game?"
- "How did the board vote, and what did it change?"

## 3. Why (15 min)

Link results to mechanics, then to concepts:

| Common pattern | Question to ask | Concept |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Big velocity gain in Q1, collapse in Q3 | "What did you borrow from the future?" | Compounding debt (Cunningham) |
| Negative cash at the end | "When did you lose control of the budget?" | Run vs change budget |
| Board rejected several times | "Who was your proposal for? Who did you forget?" | Stakeholders (Freeman), interests vs positions (Fisher & Ury) |
| Executive out of patience | "What new did you bring to each exchange?" | Credibility, cost of repetition |
| Series of incidents | "Which signals had you seen on the 3D map?" | Reliability, risk, feedback loops (Meadows) |
| Pacts signed too fast | "What did political or social peace cost you?" | Concessions and credible commitments |

## 4. And in real life? (8 min)

- "Where do you see compounding debt in your organisation?"
- "Who is your 'board', and how do you prepare it?"
- "Which decision from this game would you make differently on Monday morning?"

## 5. Closing (2 min)

Each participant writes **one lesson** and **one action** they will apply.

## Typical mistakes to surface

- Betting everything on speed in quarter 1.
- Ignoring recurring costs and only looking at investment.
- Pitching the board without preparing executives 1-on-1.
- Repeating the same proposal instead of enriching it.
- Keeping no margin for the quarter's crisis.
- Modernising a little everywhere instead of finishing projects.
