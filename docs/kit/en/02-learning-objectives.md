# Learning Objectives

## Audience

- MBA and master's students (management, strategy, information systems, engineering).
- Executive education: programme directors, architects, CIOs, CFOs, plant directors, consultants.
- No technical prerequisite: every metric is defined in the game (glossary and tooltips), and scenarios can be industrial, commercial or sourcing cases.

## What participants learn

After a session, participants can:

1. **Trade off short term and long term**: explain why an immediate speed gain (bypassing controls, underinvesting) erodes future capacity, and quantify it.
2. **Reason about compounding debt**: recognise a debt (technical, industrial, integration, dependency), estimate its carrying cost and prioritise paying it down.
3. **Separate run budget and change budget**: understand why cutting recurring costs frees investment capacity.
4. **Manage a constrained portfolio**: choose initiatives under limited budget, delivery capacity and time, sequencing investments.
5. **Map and win over stakeholders**: identify interests, biases and hidden agendas, build a proposal several camps can accept, negotiate concessions.
6. **Decide in a crisis**: respond to a shock (audit, outage, supplier failure, cyberattack) weighing cost, risk and trust.
7. **Read a system**: connect debt, reliability, incidents, costs and trust as feedback loops rather than independent metrics.

## Mapping to game mechanics

| Objective | Mechanic that makes it real |
| --- | --- |
| 1. Short / long term | Governance postures, EXTREME-risk "shortcut" initiatives |
| 2. Compounding debt | Debt index with compound interest, debt created by delivery |
| 3. Run / change | Funded run budget, overruns charged to cash |
| 4. Constrained portfolio | Budget envelope, capacity, multi-quarter initiatives |
| 5. Stakeholders | AI executives, patience, pacts, board and mandate |
| 6. Crisis | Quarterly event, facilitator-injected crises |
| 7. Systems thinking | Probabilistic incidents, diminishing returns, resilience erosion |

## Useful references for the debrief

- Ward Cunningham, the technical debt metaphor (1992).
- R. Edward Freeman, *Strategic Management: A Stakeholder Approach* (1984).
- Mitchell, Agle & Wood, stakeholder salience: power, legitimacy, urgency (1997).
- Roger Fisher & William Ury, *Getting to Yes* (1981): negotiate on interests, not positions.
- John Kotter, *Leading Change* (1996).
- Donella Meadows, *Thinking in Systems* (2008): stocks, flows and feedback loops.
